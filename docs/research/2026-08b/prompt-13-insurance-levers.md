# Prompt 13 — Commercial Trucking Insurance 2026: What Moves a 15-Truck Fleet's Premium, and What the TMS Should Surface

Date: 2026-08-08 · Fleet context: Thind Transport LLC, ~15-truck interstate dry-van carrier, Kent WA (USDOT 2523064, MC 876103) · Researcher note: all external claims tagged (search-verified 2026-08-08) unless noted; WebFetch was egress-blocked for trade press (e.g. overdriveonline.com), so page-level verification was not possible — treat single-source % figures as directional.

## TL;DR

- Commercial auto renewals are still rising in 2026, but decelerating: IVANS Index renewal rate change +6.46% (Dec 2025) → +5.62% (Jan 2026) → +4.96% (May 2026); CIAB put Q4 auto liability at +6.6%. Budget +5–8% on a flat renewal, more with a loss. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- ATRI's 2025 Operational Costs report: insurance ≈ $0.106/mile industry average (+3.9% YoY), and premiums re-accelerated +6.4% in Q1 2026. At ~100k mi/truck that is ≈ $10–11k/truck/yr — a WA long-haul dry-van fleet with clean-ish scores realistically sits ~$10k–16k/truck/yr all-lines (inference, medium confidence; WA is a high-cost "Tier 2" state, Seattle/Tacoma garaging quoted +25–30% vs eastern WA by one source, low confidence).
- Underwriters open the CAB report before anything else ("What's the DOT number?"): BASICs, OOS rates vs national averages, crash history, inspection trends, authority/insurance history, units-vs-drivers ratio. You can see your own data via CAB's MC Advantage.
- The fastest movable inputs (6–12 mo): SMS percentiles (last 6 months weighted 3x — a clean 6-month stretch moves scores), DataQs challenges (~30% modification rate), vehicle-maintenance OOS avoidance (brakes+tires = ~62% of vehicle OOS), and a documented improvement story delivered to underwriters 60–90 days pre-renewal.
- Telematics data-sharing is the cheapest discount in market: Progressive Smart Haul ≥5% (to ~15%+), Nationwide Vantage 360 Fleet 10%, Northland up to 10% or $300/truck/yr device subsidy, Sentry up to 5% at bind via TruckerCloud — which LoadOff already integrates. Insurtechs go further: Nirvana up to ~20% upfront, Cover Whale up to 30% at renewal (cam-or-ELD data connection mandatory; discount is cam-scored) (corrected on verification), HDVI Shift up to 20% monthly (Shift 2.0; first-gen was 12%) (corrected on verification).
- Cameras are the missing hardware: no camera integration exists in LoadOff yet, and dual-facing AI cams are the entry ticket to the best-priced programs and the main defense against nuclear-verdict liability claims.
- COIs: brokers/shippers want same-day certs with blanket additional-insured, waiver of subrogation, primary & non-contributory endorsements. A TMS cannot legally issue an ACORD 25 — only the agent/insurer can — but it CAN automate the request/track/deliver loop; myCOI/TrustLayer/Certificial are demand-side tools (myCOI needs 200+ incoming certs) and are not for a 15-truck carrier.
- Renewal is a 120/90/60/45/30/14-day project: docs at 120, loss runs (5-yr, "currently valued" within 30–90 days; insurers must furnish in ~10 days most states) at 90, broker markets at 60, proposals at 45, negotiate at 30, bind at 14.
- LoadOff already stores ~80% of a submission package (drivers/CDL/med-card, VINs, IFTA miles by state, maintenance records, incidents with DOT-recordable flags, DVIRs, HOS, policy expiries) — the gap is loss runs + current decs pages, which the owner checklist already requests.
- Verdict: request a Nirvana Fleet Program quote at the next renewal (10+ units = their fleet program, dry-van non-hazmat clean-record is their stated appetite); HDVI as backup quote; Cover Whale lower priority (owner-op/small-account focus, cam-or-ELD telemetry mandatory). Confirm WA availability on all three — none is page-confirmed for WA.

## 1. Market state 2025–2026

**Rate trend.** Commercial auto has been the P&C industry's problem child for over a decade:

- AM Best (Sept 2025 market segment report, via Insurance Journal): 2024 commercial auto combined ratio **107.2**, ≈ **$4.9B underwriting loss**, with **14 of the top 20** writers above 100; auto **liability** alone lost ≈ $6.4B in 2024 and insurers added another ~$2B of reserve deficiency in 2025. 2025 improved but stayed negative (~$1.9B loss, medium confidence — figure via Insurance Journal summary). (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- IVANS Index (monthly renewal-rate-change series, ~120M transactions): commercial auto **+6.46% Dec 2025 → +5.62% Jan 2026 → +4.96% May 2026**. Rising, but decelerating. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- CIAB quarterly survey (via Lockton market update, March 2026): **auto liability +6.6%** in Q4. Marsh's Q2 2026 Global Insurance Market Index shows global commercial rates falling 6% overall while **US casualty still +2%, driven by auto and excess** — transportation named as facing "far more difficult conditions" than the average insured. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- FreightWaves: trucking insurance premiums rose an average **+8.3%/yr 2017–2025**; trucking auto-liability premium per mile is up ~36% over eight years (CCJ, citing ATRI). (search-verified 2026-08-08)

## 2. What underwriters actually check

**CAB report (Central Analysis Bureau)** — the underwriter's first screen. Subscribers-only report, re-scored monthly from public data: crash records, BASIC scores, OOS rates vs national averages, inspection history and violation trends, authority and DOT safety-rating history, insurance history (including lapses/"inadequate insurance"), hazmat activity, power-unit vs driver counts and mileage (utilization sanity checks). AssuredPartners: when an agent calls an underwriter, "the first question is always, 'What's the DOT Number?'" and the CAB report comes up on screen. Shippers, brokers, and plaintiff attorneys pull the same report. Carriers can see their own data through **CAB's MC Advantage** (some insurers, e.g. Acuity, bundle free access). (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**FMCSA SMS / ISS.**
- BASIC intervention thresholds (property carrier): **65th percentile** for Unsafe Driving, Crash Indicator, HOS Compliance; **80th** for Vehicle Maintenance, Controlled Substances, Driver Fitness. Scores are relative percentiles vs peer group, not absolutes. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- **ISS score** (what roadside officers see): 1–100 from BASIC percentiles; **75–100 = "Inspect"**, 50–74 = optional, 1–49 = pass. A high ISS is a compounding tax: more inspections → more violation exposure → worse BASICs → higher ISS. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- **In flux:** FMCSA's SMS overhaul (BASICs → "compliance categories", Controlled Substances folded into Unsafe Driving, Vehicle Maintenance split in two, consolidated violation codes) is previewable now — log into the **CSA Prioritization Preview** (csa.fmcsa.dot.gov/prioritizationpreview) with the DOT PIN to see Thind's ratings under the new methodology before underwriters do. (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**OOS rates vs national averages.** 2025 CVSA International Roadcheck: **18.1% vehicle OOS, 5.9% driver OOS** across 56,178 inspections; brake defects were 41.1% of vehicle OOS orders and tires another 21.4%. (Annualized all-inspection national averages are commonly cited around ~20–22% vehicle / ~5–6% driver — low confidence, varies by source/year.) Underwriters compare the carrier's rolling rates on the CAB report against these lines; being materially above is a declination trigger. (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**Driver criteria (typical underwriting box).** Age **23+** (some markets 25+), **2+ years CDL/tractor-trailer experience**, **≤3 moving violations in 3 years**, **no DUI in 5 years**, no drug violations, no serious preventable accidents. Drivers outside the box get surcharged, excluded, or kill the quote. (search-verified 2026-08-08, aggregated from underwriting-requirement pages — exact boxes vary by market)

**Radius & commodity.** Rating buckets are roughly local (0–50 mi), regional (51–300/500), long-haul (300/500+); long-haul is rated highest for severity and time-in-transit. One short-haul program markets rates "typically 37% less" for <500-mile-radius operations (marketing claim, low confidence) — the honest takeaway is that radius is a big rating input Thind mostly cannot change while running interstate dry van, but **IFTA state-mileage data proving actual lanes** keeps the radius classification honest instead of defaulted to worst-case. Dry van general freight is already the cheapest commodity class vs reefer/hazmat/high-theft. (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**What a carrier can move in 6–12 months (broker/underwriter guidance).**

| Input | Movable in 6–12 mo? | Mechanism / magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| SMS BASIC percentiles | **Yes** | Last 6 months of events weighted 3x — a clean 6-month stretch moves percentiles more than most carriers expect; underwriters want a 12–18-month declining trend (Foley, CNS, FleetCollect). |
| Roadside/OOS record | **Yes** | Pre-trip discipline against brakes/tires/lights (~62% of vehicle OOS causes); every clean inspection improves the clean:violation ratio. |
| Bad data points | **Partly** | DataQs challenges — ~30% result in modification; evidence of active challenges itself reads well. |
| Driver roster quality | **Yes** | Replace/coach out-of-box MVRs before renewal snapshot; underwriters re-run MVRs at renewal. |
| Schedule rating | **Yes** | Underwriter discretionary **credits/debits up to ±25%** in most states for documented safety programs, telematics, maintenance — this is where a good submission literally becomes premium. |
| Loss history | **No (only ages)** | 5-year window; a bad year takes 5 years to roll off — mitigate with narrative + corrective actions. |
| Radius/commodity/garaging | **Mostly no** | Structural for an interstate dry-van fleet based in King County. |

## 4. COI operations

**What brokers/shippers demand.** Certificate naming them as holder, usually **additional insured** status (must exist as a policy endorsement — words typed on the cert alone are void), **waiver of subrogation**, and **primary & non-contributory** wording (e.g. CA T4 99 auto endorsement). Turnaround expectation is same-day; brokers routinely won't tender the first load without the cert. Same-day is realistic when three things are true: policy active, the endorsements are **blanket** ("any person or organization required by written contract") and pre-installed, and the agent has complete holder details. (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**Automation landscape.** myCOI, TrustLayer, Certificial are **demand-side** compliance platforms — built for the party *collecting* certificates (shippers, brokers, GCs). myCOI has a ~200-incoming-certificate minimum; TrustLayer is broad vendor-compliance; Certificial's "Smart COI" monitors live policy data from the agent's management system and also powers agency-side issuance. None of these is a product a 15-truck carrier buys. (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**Can a small carrier self-serve same-day COIs?** Yes, two ways: (1) many independent agencies expose a **self-service certificate portal** where the insured issues certs to pre-approved holder lists instantly — ask the agent explicitly for this at placement (it is one of Logrock's "questions that separate good agents"); (2) failing that, a standing email template with exact holder name/address/endorsement wording gets minutes-to-hours turnaround from any competent agency. The renewal question to ask: "How do you prevent COI errors and what's your issuance SLA?" (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**What LoadOff can legitimately automate** (a TMS must never generate or alter an ACORD 25 — only the agent/insurer issues certificates; a self-made cert is fraud):
1. Store the master COI + endorsement pages and auto-attach to the carrier packet — `src/lib/hub/packet.ts` already sends latest-per-kind W-9/COI/authority ("brokers don't want three stale COIs").
2. One-click **COI request email** to the agent of record (name/email already requested in docs/OWNER-CHECKLIST.md line 152) pre-filled with holder details from the customer record (`/hub/customers`), and a log of who was sent which cert when.
3. Expiry tracking + renewal countdown — compliance wall already tracks `insurance_expiry` per truck (`src/lib/hub/compliance.ts`); add the policy-level expiry with 90/60/30 nudges.
4. Portal delivery: expose the current COI on the broker-facing packet/portal so repeat customers self-serve the latest cert instead of emailing.

## 5. Renewal playbook (small fleet)

**Timeline** (search-verified 2026-08-08; SoCal Truck Ins. / Prime World / USA Specialty best-practice guides):

- **T-120d** — assemble the data room; fix roster (out-of-box MVR drivers), start clean-inspection push (6-mo 3x weighting means today's discipline shows by renewal).
- **T-90d** — order **5-year loss runs** from all current/prior insurers (they must furnish within ~10 days in most states; NY 20). Loss runs must be "currently valued" within 30–90 days of application — order fresh, not early. Update equipment schedule and driver list. Share the safety-improvement narrative with the broker.
- **T-60d** — broker markets the account. Demand the **market list in writing** (which carriers, declination reasons). This is the shop-or-bluff checkpoint.
- **T-45d** — proposals in; compare limit/deductible structures, telematics credits, schedule credits applied.
- **T-30d** — negotiate; play second-best quote against incumbent.
- **T-14d** — bind; confirm filings (BMC-91/MCS-90) and blanket endorsements are on the new policy before day 1; re-issue COIs to all active customers.

**Submission package** (what underwriters expect; each item mapped to LoadOff in Deliverable b): ACORD applications; 5-yr currently-valued loss runs; driver list with DOB/CDL#/state/hire date/years-experience/MVR status; equipment schedule with VINs + stated values; annual mileage and **IFTA state-mileage breakdown** (proves radius/territory); maintenance program description + records sample; safety program: telematics, cameras, coaching cadence, DVIR compliance, drug/alcohol program; accident register + corrective actions; DataQs activity; sometimes financials for larger schedules.

**Questions that force a broker to actually shop** (Logrock "9 questions" + renewal guides, search-verified 2026-08-08):
1. Are you independent or captive — how many trucking markets are you appointed with?
2. Exactly which markets will you submit to, and will you show me the responses/declinations?
3. What class/radius/commodity are you submitting me as? (Wrong defaults = silent overcharge.)
4. What schedule credits are you requesting, citing my telematics/maintenance/DVIR documentation, and what did the underwriter grant? (±25% discretionary swing.)
5. What deductible options were quoted (AL and APD) and the premium delta for each?
6. Which telematics/dashcam programs am I eligible for with my existing stack (TruckerCloud-connected ELD)?
7. When do you start renewal marketing, and what's your COI issuance SLA / do you offer self-service certs?

## Deliverable (c) — Insurtech verdict for a 15-truck WA dry-van fleet

**Worth a quote request: Nirvana (primary), HDVI (backup). Cover Whale: lower priority.**

- **Nirvana — YES, request at T-90.** Fleet Program is explicitly **10+ power units** (up to ~200), stated appetite = dry van/reefer/flatbed, non-hazmat, clean records — Thind is the target profile. Up to ~20% upfront savings priced from connected ELD data (Samsara, Motive, Verizon Connect and other majors); telematics not strictly required to bind but data-sharers get the pricing; A-rated paper. **Caveat:** coverage spans 26–27 states as of early 2026 and WA was not individually confirmed in any page I could verify — first question for the broker. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- **HDVI — backup quote.** Shift product re-prices monthly, **up to 20% off** (Shift 2.0; first-gen 12%) (corrected on verification) for safe driving; telematics device required (HDVI-provided or compatible existing); sweet spot is mid-size fleets on Spinnaker paper — 15 trucks is at the small end but inside range. Good pressure quote even if not bound. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- **Cover Whale — lower priority.** Agent-distributed, strongest for owner-operators/1–10-truck and harder-to-place accounts; **a Cover Whale dashcam or existing-ELD connection is mandatory on every AL policy (corrected on verification)** (Thind has no cams today), discounts (up to 30%) earned at renewal through cam-scored behavior. Reconsider after lever 3 (cams installed) or if standard markets decline. (search-verified 2026-08-08)
- **Loadsure — not a fleet-premium play**, but a legitimate LoadOff product feature: per-load all-risk cargo top-up (≤$2M/shipment) bound in seconds via API, already embedded in McLeod TMS — the pattern LoadOff could copy for high-value loads above the $100k cargo policy. (search-verified 2026-08-08)

**Telematics requirements summary:** Nirvana = connect existing ELD (aggregator-friendly); HDVI = telematics device (theirs or compatible); Cover Whale = dashcam-or-ELD data connection, non-negotiable (dual-facing cams score the deepest discounts) (corrected on verification).
