## 3. Compliance constraints on the ads

### 3a. Washington pay transparency — RCW 49.58.110 (ESSB 5761; amended by SB 5408, eff. July 27, 2025)
(search-verified 2026-08-08) [7][8][9][10][11]
- **Who's covered:** employers with **15 or more employees** engaging in business in WA. Thind at ~15 drivers plus any office staff should assume it is covered. Count all employees.
- **What every posting must contain (near-verbatim):** *"the wage scale or salary range, and a general description of all of the benefits and other compensation to be offered to the hired applicant"* — except where only a **fixed wage amount** is offered, in which case disclose the fixed amount instead of a range (fixed-wage option added 2025).
- **What counts as a "posting" (near-verbatim):** *"any solicitation intended to recruit job applicants for a specific available position, including recruitment done directly by an employer or indirectly through a third party, and includes any postings done electronically, or with a printed hard copy, that includes qualifications for desired applicants."* → Indeed/Zip/Craigslist listings and **gurdwara flyers listing qualifications are postings**. A spoken radio spot is arguably not (it isn't electronic-posted or printed text), but the safe pattern is: radio drives to the website posting, which is compliant (inference).
- **Third-party reposts:** you are responsible for postings made *"directly or indirectly through a third party"*; the 2025 amendment excludes postings **scraped/replicated without your consent** from liability.
- **Teeth:** applicant/employee may go through L&I (civil penalty up to $1,000 plus statutory damages) **or sue directly for statutory damages of $100–$5,000 per violation plus attorneys' fees**. **Cure period:** anyone may send written notice of a defective posting; you get **5 business days** to fix it (and instruct third-party boards to fix it) and owe nothing if you do — this safe harbor **sunsets July 27, 2027**.
- **Owner-operators (1099):** the statute speaks to employees/job applicants; coverage of true independent-contractor solicitations is unresolved in the guidance found. **Verdict: include the compensation terms (percentage, FSC, settlement cadence) in O/O ads anyway** — it costs nothing, matches Truth-in-Leasing substance, and avoids arguing about classification in an ad.

### 3b. Earnings-claims risk ("up to $X")
- **FTC v. Lyft (Oct 2024):** $2.1M penalty for advertising "up to $X/hr" figures achieved only by the top ~20% of drivers — the FTC's position is that "up to" earnings claims must reflect what's *typically* achievable (search-verified 2026-08-08) [21].
- **Super Ego Holding:** driver class action built directly on recruiting ads "guaranteeing 88% of every load" and "3,000–4,000 miles per week" [22]. **C.R. England:** ~$37M class settlement over driver-recruitment/training misrepresentations [23].
- **Rules for Thind ad copy:** advertise **ranges with their basis** (rate × realistic miles), never "up to" a best-case; every number must trace to `PAY_RATES` (the repo's test suite enforces exactly this); "90% of gross" and "100% fuel surcharge" must match the written lease **word-for-word** — 49 CFR 376.12 requires the lease to state compensation clearly, and O/Os can sue privately under Truth-in-Leasing [46][47]. Avoid all lease-purchase framing (FMCSA's Truck Leasing Task Force, Jan 2025, found common arrangements predatory) [48].

### 3c. FMCSA / English Language Proficiency (the 2025–26 change)
- 49 CFR 391.11(b)(2) requires drivers to read/speak English sufficiently; **since June 25, 2025 an ELP failure is an out-of-service violation** (CVSA criteria, per Executive Order 14286), with **12,308 OOS violations in H2 2025**; Congress mandated codification and FMCSA's **proposed** rule (NPRM; comments due Oct 9, 2026) publishes in the Federal Register **Aug 10, 2026** (corrected on verification — proposed, not final) (search-verified 2026-08-08) [27][28][29].
- **Ad implications:** never imply English isn't needed ("Punjabi only OK" = recruiting drivers into OOS risk); it is fine — and honest — to say interviews/orientation/dispatch are available in Punjabi while stating drivers must meet all FMCSA qualifications including English proficiency. FMCSA has **no rule governing recruiting-ad content itself** (verdict — none found; general FTC/state UDAP law fills that space).

### 3d. EEOC and language-targeted advertising
- Title VII bars ads *indicating preference by national origin*. **Language is not national origin per se**: a fluency requirement/preference is lawful **only if required for effective job performance, assessed case-by-case** (EEOC Enforcement Guidance on National Origin Discrimination) (search-verified 2026-08-08) [24][25].
- For a driver role, "Punjabi-speaking **required**" is hard to justify as job-related (the job's language demands are English-facing: signs, inspections, shippers). "Punjabi-speaking **preferred**" is the pattern local carriers use (e.g., Bellevue Trucking's Kent ad) and sits in a gray zone — defensible if genuinely about dispatch communication, attackable as an ethnic proxy.
- **The climate raised the stakes:** EEOC's Nov 19, 2025 technical assistance ("Discrimination Against American Workers Is Against the Law"; date corrected on verification) plus DOL's "Project Firewall" target ads preferring any national-origin/visa group; the EEOC's own example of unlawful copy is ads *"preferring or requiring applicants from a particular country or with a particular visa status"* [26]. Word-of-mouth-only recruiting from a homogeneous workforce is also a long-flagged EEOC concern (moderate confidence) [24].
- **Verdict — the compliant formula:** (1) say **"Dispatch, orientation, and paperwork help available in Punjabi and English"** — a true statement about the workplace, not a screen on applicants; (2) drop "Punjabi preferred" from all copy; (3) run the same job on at least one general-audience channel (Indeed/Craigslist) whenever it runs on Punjabi channels, so *placement* targeting is outreach, not exclusion; (4) put a standard EEO line on every posting.

### B. Compliance checklist for the ad copy

Before anything ships, every posting passes all of these:

1. **[WA-EPOA] Wage scale or salary range present** — or a single fixed wage if that's genuinely the only offer (RCW 49.58.110; fixed-wage option per 2025 SB 5408). Pull from `PAY_RATES`; never retype numbers by hand [7][9].
2. **[WA-EPOA] "General description of all of the benefits and other compensation"** present — sign-on bonus, weekly direct deposit, performance/referral bonuses, home-time options (mirror `BENEFITS`) [7].
3. **[WA-EPOA] Applies to every "posting"** — Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Craigslist, the website, **and printed gurdwara flyers that list qualifications**. Radio scripts should point to the compliant web posting rather than carry qualifications alone (inference) [8].
4. **[WA-EPOA] Third-party listings you place are your responsibility**; auto-scraped copies you didn't consent to are not. If anyone sends written notice of a defect: **fix within 5 business days** (safe harbor until 2027-07-27); exposure otherwise is $100–$5,000 per violation + fees [9][10].
5. **[FTC/UDAP] No "up to $X."** Ranges with basis only ("$X.XX/mile; drivers running [N] miles/wk gross $[range]"). Every figure must be typical, current, and sourced from `PAY_RATES` (Lyft order; Super Ego; C.R. England) [21][22][23].
6. **[Truth-in-Leasing] O/O ads must match the lease**: same percentage, same 100% FSC pass-through, same settlement cadence as the written §376.12 lease. No lease-purchase offers or framing [46][47][48].
7. **[EEOC] No national-origin or visa-status preference**, no "Punjabi preferred/required." Use: *"Dispatch, orientation, and paperwork help available in Punjabi and English."* Standard EEO line on every posting. Punjabi-channel placement always paired with ≥1 general channel [24][25][26].
8. **[FMCSA ELP] State the requirement, don't waive it**: "Must meet all FMCSA driver qualifications, including English proficiency (49 CFR 391.11(b)(2))." Never "English not needed" [27][28].
9. **[Repo rule] No unverifiable superlatives** — no "top pay," "best in WA," "industry leading" (the constants file's "Industry leading!" string should not travel into ads). Only claims backed by `constants.ts` or public record (USDOT/MC, founded 2014, 15 trucks, 48 states).
10. **[Repo rule] Punjabi is never machine-translated** — English master → family translator → reviewed native script (see hand-off note below).
